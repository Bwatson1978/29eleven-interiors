How we source: finding one-of-a-kind pieces for real homes

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

One of the most common questions we hear from clients is: where do you find this stuff? The honest answer is: everywhere. + it’s one of the parts of this job we love most. Sourcing — the process of finding the exact right piece for the exact right moment in a home — is where design goes from concept to reality.

Why Sourcing Matters

You can design two identical rooms on paper + have them feel completely different based on what’s actually in them. A mass-produced coffee table from a catalog versus a hand-carved one from a small workshop in Oaxaca. A machine-made rug versus a hand-knotted one with visible irregularities that tell you a human made it.

The difference isn’t snobbishness. It’s energy. Handmade pieces carry a quality that manufactured ones don’t, + your eye picks up on it even if you can’t articulate why.

Our Sourcing Process

For every project, we build what we call a sourcing deck. It starts with the design concept + breaks down every room into categories: seating, tables, lighting, rugs, art, accessories, + textiles. Each category gets its own hunt.

We pull from a mix of sources. Trade-only showrooms give us access to lines the public doesn’t see. Vintage dealers + estate sales provide one-of-a-kind finds with history. Artisan studios let us commission custom pieces. + yes, we also use well-designed retail pieces when they’re the right fit.

The goal is never to source the most expensive thing. It’s to source the most right thing.

— Ashley Porter

The Art of the Mix

A room where everything is new looks like a showroom. A room where everything is vintage looks like a time capsule. The magic is in the mix.

In a typical 29 Eleven living room, you might find a contemporary sofa from an Italian line, accent chairs reupholstered from a 1960s estate sale find, a coffee table from a small woodworker in Joshua Tree, table lamps from a ceramics studio in Portugal, + a vintage rug from Turkey. On paper, it sounds chaotic. In person, it feels cohesive because every piece shares a material warmth + a human quality that ties them together.

Shoppe: Sharing What We Find

This love of sourcing is what led us to create Shoppe — our curated shopping experience where we share the pieces we’re excited about with clients + design enthusiasts. Every item is something we’d put in our own projects.

It’s our way of making the sourcing process accessible to people who love design but don’t necessarily need a full-service designer. You can shop the aesthetic without the project.
