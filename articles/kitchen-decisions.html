The 5 decisions that define a luxury kitchen renovation

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

Every kitchen renovation involves hundreds of decisions. Paint sheen. Grout color. Drawer pull spacing. It’s easy to get lost in the details + lose sight of the decisions that actually define whether your kitchen feels extraordinary or just expensive. After designing kitchens across Southern California, I’ve found that five decisions shape everything else.

Decision One: The Stone

Your countertop is the single most visually dominant surface in a kitchen. It sets the tone for everything — cabinetry color, backsplash choice, hardware finish, even the shade of white on your walls.

I almost always advocate for natural stone. Not because quartz is bad — it’s incredibly practical — but because natural stone has movement, depth, + character that engineered surfaces can’t replicate. Every slab is one of one. Choose your stone first. Visit the slab yard in person. Then build the rest of the kitchen around that slab.

Decision Two: The Layout

No amount of beautiful finishes can fix a kitchen that doesn’t flow. The key question isn’t “work triangle or no work triangle.” It’s this: how do you actually use your kitchen? Do you cook alone or with a partner? Do your kids sit at the island while you prep?

A luxury kitchen isn’t just one that looks good in photos. It’s one that makes cooking dinner on a Wednesday feel effortless.

Decision Three: The Cabinetry Profile

Cabinets occupy more visual real estate than any other element. A flat-panel door in white oak reads modern + warm. The same layout in beaded inset reads classic + refined. A slab door in matte lacquer reads sleek + minimal.

In our projects, we often mix profiles: flat panels on uppers, a more detailed profile on the island, open shelving in the pantry. This layering creates depth + keeps the kitchen from feeling like a single note.

Decision Four: The Lighting Layers

Lighting is the most underestimated element in kitchen design. A well-lit kitchen has three layers: ambient light from overhead fixtures for general illumination, task lighting under cabinets + over the island for actual cooking, + accent lighting that adds atmosphere in the evening.

The pendant lights over your island get all the attention, but the quality of light in your kitchen comes from the combination of all three layers working together.

Decision Five: The Hardware

Hardware is the jewelry of a kitchen. It’s the element you touch most often, + it has an outsized impact on whether the space reads cohesive or disjointed.

My personal favorite right now: unlacquered brass. It starts shiny + develops a patina over months of use, making the kitchen feel more authentic over time. It’s a small detail that signals a certain confidence in the design.

The Takeaway

Every decision after these five becomes easier because the foundation is set. The backsplash, the paint color, the bar stools — they all take their cues from the stone, the layout, the cabinets, the lighting, + the hardware. Master the five, + you’ll love your kitchen for decades.
