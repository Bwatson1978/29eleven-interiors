What your builder won’t tell you about design timelines

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

Here’s something I wish every homeowner building a custom home knew: by the time most people call an interior designer, dozens of decisions have already been made that will limit what design can do. Not because the builder did anything wrong. But because builders build — + designers design. The timelines are different, + nobody talks about that.

The Timeline Gap Nobody Mentions

In a typical custom build, here’s what happens: the architect draws plans, the builder prices them, permits get pulled, + framing starts. Somewhere around the time drywall goes up, someone says, “We should probably call a designer.”

By that point, the electrical plan is locked. The plumbing is roughed in. The niche dimensions are set. The ceiling heights are final. + the designer walks into a project where the biggest, most impactful decisions — the ones that cost the least to change early + the most to change later — are already done.

From the Studio

We’ve saved clients tens of thousands of dollars simply by reviewing plans before framing begins. Moving a wall on paper costs nothing. Moving it after drywall costs everything.

What Changes When a Designer Is There from Day One

When we’re brought in during the architectural planning phase, we influence decisions that no amount of decorating can fix later. Outlet placement. The height + width of windows. Where the pantry door swings. Whether the shower niche is 12 inches or 16. Whether the kitchen island is 10 feet or 11.

These sound small. They’re not. An outlet in the wrong place means a lamp cord crossing a floor. A shower niche that’s too narrow means your shampoo bottles don’t fit. A kitchen island that’s one foot too short means two people can’t prep side by side.

The Real Timeline: What to Expect

During schematic design, we’re reviewing floor plans for flow, functionality, + furniture placement. We’re flagging rooms that are too narrow for the furniture our clients will want. We’re coordinating with the architect on ceiling details, niche placements, + built-in dimensions.

During construction documents, we’re specifying all finish materials: tile, stone, cabinetry, hardware, plumbing fixtures, lighting. This is when selections need to happen — not when the tile installer is standing in the driveway waiting.

During construction, we’re doing site visits to verify installations match intent. After substantial completion, we’re installing furniture, hanging art, styling shelves, + making the house feel like home.

Build Smart, Design Early

The best homes we’ve worked on are the ones where the designer + builder were partners from the beginning. Not because we need to control the process — but because design decisions compound. A good early decision makes every subsequent decision easier + better.

If you’re planning a build, bring your designer to the first meeting. If you’re already building, bring them to the next one. Your future self will thank you.
