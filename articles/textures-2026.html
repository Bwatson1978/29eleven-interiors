Beyond bouclé: the textures + materials we’re reaching for in 2026

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

Bouclé had a good run. For three or four years, that nubby, cream-colored textile was everywhere — on dining chairs, accent chairs, sofas, headboards, even pillows you’d be afraid to actually lean on. + look, it was beautiful. But like everything that saturates a market, it stopped feeling special.

Where Texture Is Heading

The shift we’re seeing in 2026 isn’t from one trendy texture to another. It’s a move toward materiality — a deeper interest in what things are actually made of, not just what they look like.

Clients are asking smarter questions. Where was this woven? Is this solid wood or veneer? Will this patina or just degrade? That sophistication is driving the entire market toward honest materials with real character.

Mohair + Alpaca: Warmth with Edge

Mohair is having a serious moment, + it deserves it. Where bouclé read soft + approachable, mohair reads rich + slightly wild. The pile catches light differently depending on the angle, giving furniture a dimensional quality that flat weaves can’t match.

We’ve been using mohair on accent chairs in saturated tones — deep olive, warm cognac, slate blue. Alpaca, mohair’s gentler cousin, works beautifully in throws + pillows. It’s softer than cashmere, hypoallergenic, + has a subtle sheen that reads luxury without trying.

Limewash + Plaster Finishes

If there’s one finish we’re using more than any other in 2026, it’s limewash. On walls, on range hoods, on fireplace surrounds. Where paint is opaque + uniform, limewash is translucent + varies with every brushstroke. It looks like a surface that has lived, even when it’s brand new.

We pair limewash walls with raw plaster details — sculptural shelving, organic-shaped mirrors with plaster frames, textured ceiling treatments. The combination reads European + timeless without veering into pastiche.

Warm Metals: Beyond Brushed Brass

Brass isn’t going anywhere, but the finish is evolving. The heavily brushed, Instagram-golden brass of 2022 is being replaced by quieter treatments: unlacquered brass that develops real patina, oil-rubbed bronze with warm undertones, + aged iron with a copper wash.

We’re also seeing a return of mixed metals done confidently. The key is intentionality — mixing metals reads sophisticated when it looks deliberate + messy when it looks accidental.

What This Means for Your Home

The common thread across all of these shifts is authenticity. The materials + textures defining 2026 are ones that age gracefully, feel good under your hands, + look better with time. If you’re starting a project this year, lean into real materials. Your home will thank you for it in ten years.
