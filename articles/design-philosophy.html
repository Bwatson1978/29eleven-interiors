Why your home should feel like a deep breath

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

There’s a moment in every project that tells me we got it right. It’s not when the last pillow is placed or the final photograph is taken. It’s the first time a client walks into their finished home, stops in the doorway, + exhales. Not a gasp. Not a wow. An exhale. That unconscious release of tension that happens when your body recognizes it’s somewhere safe, somewhere that fits.

What I Mean by “Design That Breathes”

I get asked about my design style constantly — at consultations, at dinner parties, in Instagram DMs. + I’ve never been great at the elevator pitch. Coastal modern? Sure, sometimes. Transitional? That’s a word that means everything + nothing. California casual? Closer, but still not quite it.

What I’ve landed on, after years of trying to articulate it, is this: I design homes that breathe. Spaces with room to exhale. Rooms where nothing feels forced, where the materials feel inevitable rather than chosen, where the palette feels like it grew there rather than being applied.

Every home should feel like a deep breath — grounding, warm, + unmistakably yours.

— Ashley Porter

Warmth Is Not a Color — It’s a Feeling

One of the biggest misconceptions in design is that warmth comes from warm colors. It doesn’t. I’ve designed rooms in cool grays + soft blues that feel warmer than rooms painted in terracotta. Warmth comes from texture. From the way light hits a hand-plastered wall versus a flat-painted one. From the weight of a linen curtain versus a polyester panel. From a floor that has grain + variation versus one that’s uniform + perfect.

At 29 Eleven, we chase warmth through materiality. We source pieces that have been touched by human hands — hand-thrown ceramics, hand-loomed textiles, hand-carved wood. Not because handmade is trendy, but because your eye can tell the difference, even if your brain can’t name it.

Intention Over Trend

Trends are fine. I follow them. I appreciate them. I’ll even use one when it serves the project. But I will never design a room around a trend, because trends have expiration dates + your home shouldn’t.

Every design decision at 29 Eleven starts with the same question: why? Why this stone + not that one? Why place the sofa here + not there? Why brass + not nickel? If the answer is “because it’s trending,” we keep looking. If the answer connects to how the room feels, how the client lives, or how the light works in that specific space — that’s when we commit.

This is what separates decoration from design. Decoration fills a room. Design shapes how it feels to live in one.

Designing for the Exhale

So when you ask what 29 Eleven’s design philosophy is, this is it. We design for the exhale. We want your home to lower your shoulders when you walk in. We want the light, the texture, the temperature of the space to tell your nervous system that you’re home — really home.

That’s not something you achieve with a mood board or a Pinterest save. It’s something you build, material by material, decision by decision, with a designer who listens more than they talk + who cares more about how you feel in a room than how the room photographs.

That’s the work. + it’s the work we love.
