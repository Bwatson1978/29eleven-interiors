Lake Arrowhead: when mountain living meets modern warmth

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

When a client asks us to design a mountain home, the temptation is obvious: lean into the cabin. Exposed logs, antler chandeliers, plaid everything. But that’s not what our Lake Arrowhead clients wanted, + frankly, it’s not what the space deserved. They asked for warmth without kitsch. Modern without cold. A home that felt like the mountains without being a costume.

Reading the Room — + the Landscape

Lake Arrowhead sits at 5,100 feet. The light is different up here — sharper, cooler, filtered through pine canopies that shift with the seasons. That light became our first design decision. Everything we chose had to look as good in January’s blue-gray mornings as it did in August’s golden afternoons.

We pulled our material palette directly from what was visible through the windows: the charcoal of wet bark, the warm honey of afternoon sun on pine needles, the soft gray-green of sage brush in the ravine below. The home’s interior would mirror its exterior, not compete with it.

The Great Room: Designed for Gathering

The great room is where this project comes alive. Double-height ceilings with exposed structural beams — real ones, not decorative — give the space its drama. We kept the beams natural, sealed but unstained, so the wood’s character comes through honestly.

Below, a full-wall stone fireplace anchors the room. We chose a stacked ledger stone in a mix of warm grays that reads organic without feeling heavy. The mantle is a single slab of live-edge walnut, hand-selected for its grain.

Furnishings are oversized + sink-in comfortable. A deep sectional in a charcoal performance velvet faces the fireplace, flanked by woven leather accent chairs. The rug — hand-knotted wool in a subtle geometric — ties the seating area together + adds warmth underfoot against the heated concrete floors.

Kitchen + Dining: Mountain Casual

The kitchen runs along one wall of the open-plan ground floor, anchored by a massive island topped in honed black granite. The contrast against white oak cabinetry is deliberate — it gives the room weight without closing it in.

The dining table seats ten. We chose a solid walnut trestle table paired with upholstered dining chairs in a heathered oatmeal fabric. No precious materials here — everything in this room was selected to handle real use, real spills, real life. That’s mountain living done right.

Bridging Two Worlds

Lake Arrowhead proved something we’d long believed: you don’t have to choose between modern design + mountain warmth. They’re not opposites. When you let the environment lead — when you source honestly + design with restraint — the result is a home that feels both current + timeless.

This project expanded what 29 Eleven means. We’re a coastal firm by origin, but our design language translates anywhere there’s a beautiful setting + a client who wants their home to honor it.
