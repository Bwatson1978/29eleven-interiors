Designing for California light: how coastal living shapes our material palette

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

If you’ve ever held a paint swatch up to a wall in the morning + loved it, then come back at 4 PM + wondered what went wrong, you understand the problem. Color is not fixed. It’s a product of light. + in coastal Southern California, the light is unlike anywhere else in the country.

What Makes California Light Different

Newport Beach sits at 33 degrees latitude, facing southwest over the Pacific. The light here is intense but not harsh, warm in the morning, slightly cool + silver near midday as marine layer burns off, then deeply golden in the late afternoon.

It’s also reflected. Ocean light bounces. It fills rooms from unexpected angles. Designing for this light isn’t optional here — it’s the single most important variable in every material decision we make.

How Light Changes Stone

We’ve learned the hard way that a slab that looks perfect in a showroom can read completely different once it’s installed in a coastal home. Stones with cool blue undertones can go icy in Newport’s midday light. Warmer stones with golden veining can look almost orange in the afternoon blaze.

Our solution: we always request samples + live with them in the actual space for at least 48 hours before committing. We check them in morning light, midday light, evening light, + under fixtures at night.

The White Problem

Every designer in Southern California has a white paint story. In strong light, a pure white can look sterile + blue. A warm white can turn yellow. A greige can look pink.

Over the years, we’ve developed a shortlist of whites that reliably work in coastal light. They tend to have the slightest warm undertone — just enough to counteract the cool bounce of ocean light without reading yellow.

Textiles in Sunlight

Fabric selection in a coastal home is a UV question as much as an aesthetic one. Rooms with southern or western exposure get hammered with direct sunlight for hours. A silk pillow will fade within months. A dark velvet sofa will show sun damage within a year.

We specify performance fabrics + UV-resistant textiles in every sun-exposed room. The technology has come far enough that performance fabrics no longer feel like performance fabrics — many are indistinguishable from their natural counterparts.

Designing With Light, Not Against It

The best California homes don’t fight the light. They use it. We design material palettes that shift beautifully across the day — limewashed walls that look soft + cool in the morning + warm + textured in the evening. Oak floors whose grain becomes more visible as the sun moves across them. Brass hardware that catches the last light of day.

This is what we mean when we say we design for how a room feels. Not how it looks at one frozen moment, but how it lives across hours, seasons, + years. In California, the light does half the design work for you. You just have to listen to it.
