Indoor-outdoor living done right: lessons from Mesa Del Mar

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

In Southern California, the line between inside + outside isn’t just blurred — it barely exists. We live outdoors here. We eat outdoors, we work outdoors, we host outdoors. + yet most homes treat their outdoor spaces as afterthoughts. Mesa Del Mar changed how we think about that line.

The Brief: One Continuous Experience

Our Mesa Del Mar clients came to us with a clear vision: they didn’t want an indoor home + an outdoor home. They wanted one home that happened to have some rooms without ceilings. The transition from interior to exterior should feel like walking from one room to the next.

That brief reshaped every material + design decision we made. If the outdoor living room was going to feel like an extension of the indoor one, the quality had to match. The details had to match. The intention had to match.

The Threshold Moment

The most important square foot in any indoor-outdoor home is the threshold — the point where inside becomes outside. At Mesa Del Mar, we used full-height, multi-slide glass doors that pocket completely into the wall. When open, there’s nothing between the living room + the covered patio except a subtle change in flooring material.

Inside: wide-plank white oak. Outside: a porcelain tile that reads like natural limestone but handles weather, pool splash, + bare feet. The color + scale were matched so precisely that your eye flows across the threshold without stumbling.

Furnishing the Outdoor Room

We furnished Mesa Del Mar’s outdoor spaces the way we furnish indoor ones: with intention, layers, + a mix of materials.

The outdoor spaces at Mesa Del Mar aren’t furnished for function. They’re furnished for living. There’s a difference.

— Ashley Porter

A deep sectional in Sunbrella fabric that’s genuinely comfortable. Teak side tables with a weathered finish. A concrete coffee table. Ceramic table lamps wired for outdoor use that create actual ambiance at night. The outdoor dining area seats twelve under a pergola with retractable shade.

Material Lessons from Mesa Del Mar

We chose materials that age gracefully: teak that silvers over time, concrete that develops character, brass fixtures that patina beautifully. Nothing in the outdoor spaces is trying to stay perfect. Everything is designed to look better with use.

That philosophy — that the passage of time should improve a space, not diminish it — is central to how we approach every project, indoors + out.

Making It Work for Your Home

You don’t need a Mesa Del Mar-sized budget to get indoor-outdoor living right. Match your material tones across the threshold. Invest in outdoor furniture that’s genuinely comfortable, not just weather-resistant. Add real lighting. + think about the transition — what does it feel like to step from inside to outside? If it feels like leaving a room, something’s off. If it feels like entering one, you’ve nailed it.

Southern California gives us one of the greatest gifts in residential design: the ability to live outside, comfortably, for most of the year. The least we can do is design those spaces with the same care we give the rooms with roofs.
