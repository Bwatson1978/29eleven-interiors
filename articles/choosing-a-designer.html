How to choose an interior designer in Newport Beach

Ashley Porter Principal Designer, 29 Eleven Interiors

Choosing an interior designer is one of the most personal decisions you’ll make for your home. You’re not just hiring someone to pick paint colors. You’re inviting someone into your private life — your bedroom, your kitchen, the spaces where your family is most themselves. The right designer makes that process feel exciting. The wrong one makes it feel like a chore.

Start with the Portfolio, But Don’t Stop There

Every designer will show you their best work. But don’t just ask whether you like their aesthetic. Ask whether their work has range. Can they do a beach house + a mountain cabin? If every project in their portfolio looks identical, that tells you something: you’re going to get their style, not yours.

Look for designers who listen. Whose work reflects their clients, not just themselves. The best portfolios have a consistent quality — a sense of cohesion + care — but the individual rooms look like different people live there. Because they do.

Ask About Their Process, Not Just Their Price

Price matters. Of course it does. But the cheapest designer isn’t the best value. What you want to understand is process. How do they handle selections? How many revision rounds are included? What happens when something goes wrong with a vendor?

A designer who can clearly explain their process is a designer who’s done it enough times to have systems. Systems mean fewer surprises. + in home design, fewer surprises is worth its weight in marble.

The Newport Beach Factor

Designing in Newport Beach comes with its own set of considerations. Coastal light changes everything — materials that look warm in a showroom can read cold in a room flooded with Pacific light. Salt air affects finishes. HOA restrictions add layers of approval.

A designer who knows Newport understands these things intuitively. They’ve already learned which stones hold up near the coast, which fabrics resist fading in direct sunlight, + which vendors deliver on time to the area. That local knowledge saves you time, money, + headaches.

Chemistry Matters More Than You Think

You’re going to spend months with your designer. Sometimes a year or more. You’ll be making hundreds of decisions together, navigating budget conversations, + working through the inevitable moments when something doesn’t go as planned.

The best designer-client relationships feel like collaborations, not transactions. + that chemistry has to be real from day one.

— Ashley Porter

Trust your gut. The consultation isn’t just about seeing their ideas. It’s about feeling their energy. Do they listen before they talk? Do they ask about your life before they ask about your budget? Do they make you feel excited about the project or overwhelmed by it?

The Best First Step

The best way to find your designer is to reach out, have a real conversation, + see how it feels. No commitment required. Just a conversation about your space, your vision, + what’s possible. That’s how every great project starts.
